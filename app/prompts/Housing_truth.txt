SECTION 4.  WHAT "CONFIRMED PREDICTOR" MEANS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A confirmed predictor is a respondent characteristic that:
  (a) was selected by LASSO penalised regression (Step 3),
  (b) passed a likelihood-ratio test in the full multivariate model (Step 5),
  (c) passed Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at q = 0.05, AND
  (d) survived the events-per-predictor cap to avoid overfitting.

The Housing survey collected over 30 candidate variables.  Most were
filtered out.  Only the predictors listed in SECTION 5 passed all four
gates.  When asked "what predicts concern for hazard X", report ONLY
the predictors listed for that hazard in SECTION 5 (or state that
the hazard has no confirmed predictors).

Q: What are the affected profiles for hazard X?  (or: Who is most
   concerned about hazard X?)
A: Same as the previous question.  The affected profiles are exactly
   the confirmed predictors listed in SECTION 5 for that hazard.
   For example, for Hazard 6 (Cold/damp leads to health problems):
   - Respondents in the lowest housing-cost band: 2.5x the odds
   - Respondents in the highest housing-cost band: 2x the odds
   - Respondents aged 45+ are LESS concerned (protective)
   - Plus several country-level effects

Q: What is the most concerning Housing hazard?
A: Heating and cooling costs increase, with mean concern 14.14/25 and
   68.2% of respondents in the high-concern group.

Q: What is the strongest single finding in Housing?
A: The lowest housing-cost band predicts higher concern across four
   different Housing hazards (cold/damp-health, home insurance, damp/mould,
   power cuts).  The strongest single dummy-level coefficient for the
   "1_Low" housing-cost band is in the home_insurance model (OR = 2.81,
   p = 0.0006).  This is the most robust confirmed cross-hazard finding
   in the Housing sector.

Q: Are there country differences in Housing concern?
A: Yes.  The ANOVA is statistically significant (F=5.52, p=0.0045).
   Portuguese respondents report higher overall Housing concern than
   German respondents (Tukey p=0.005).  Housing is the ONLY sector in
   the FITTER-EU study where country differences in overall mean
   concern are statistically significant.

Q: Is X (some variable) a confirmed predictor of Housing concern?
A: Check SECTION 5 for each hazard in turn.  If X is not listed as a
   confirmed predictor under any hazard, say:
   "The variable X was considered by the model but was not confirmed
   as a predictor of any  hazard in this study."

Q: How was concern measured?
A: Each hazard was scored as Likelihood (1 to 5) multiplied by Impact
   (1 to 5), giving a 1 to 25 score.  A score of 12 or above is
   classified as high concern.

Q: What is the sample size?  Who took part?
A: 263 respondents in total: 197 from Germany (74.9%), 49 from Portugal
   (18.6%), 17 from Ireland (6.5%).  The sample is heavily German-weighted.

Q: What does "confirmed predictor" mean?
A: A confirmed predictor is one that survived four statistical filters:
   (1) LASSO penalised selection, (2) likelihood-ratio testing in the
   full multivariate model, (3) Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at
   q=0.05, and (4) the events-per-predictor cap to avoid overfitting.
   Only confirmed predictors should be reported as findings.

Q: Why do so many Housing hazards have no confirmed predictors?
A: Housing has the smallest sample (n=263) among the three sectors and
   the most hazards (13).  With smaller samples, LASSO and the FDR
   correction filter out weaker signals more aggressively.  Seven of
   the 13 hazards have no respondent characteristic that survives the
   confirmation gate.

Q: Can I say X causes Y?
A: No.  This is an observational, cross-sectional analysis.  Use
   associational language only.

Q: What about Energy or Transport?
A: This source-of-truth file covers Housing only.  Refer to the Energy
   or Transport file for those sectors.

SECTION 10.  TECHNICAL CAVEATS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A.  The Housing sample (n=263) is the smallest of the three sectors,
    and is 75% German.  Findings reflect this composition.

B.  Step 5 LRT tests a predictor in the FULL multivariate context.
    Step 7 ANOVA tests it in ISOLATION.  Disagreement between them is
    expected and not a bug.

C.  Post-LASSO p-values are slightly anti-conservative.  Treat them
    as approximate indicators of association rather than exact
    Type I error rates.

D.  Several EPP-flagged effects in Housing: solar_install_likelihood
    "Not sure" in the home_insurance model;
    heat_fuel_wood in the heat_cool model; heat_fuel_bottle_gas in
    the power_cuts model; dwelling_year "Before 1945" in the
    home_insurance model.  These are real but the precise odds-ratio
    values are uncertain because of small subgroup sizes.

E.  Direction is robust across all Housing findings (sign consistency
    averages 0.94 across confirmed predictors), but the strength of
    individual effects under resampling is weak.  Read Housing findings
    at the construct level (housing-cost burden, dampness, dwelling
    quality) rather than as individual point estimates.
