SECTION 4.  WHAT "CONFIRMED PREDICTOR" MEANS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A confirmed predictor is a respondent characteristic that:
  (a) was selected by LASSO penalised regression (Step 3),
  (b) passed a likelihood-ratio test in the full multivariate model (Step 5),
  (c) passed Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at q = 0.05, AND
  (d) survived the events-per-predictor cap to avoid overfitting.

The Energy survey collected over 30 candidate variables.  Most were
filtered out.  Only the predictors listed in SECTION 5 passed all four
gates.  When asked "what predicts concern for hazard X", report ONLY
the predictors listed for that hazard in SECTION 5.

Q: What are the affected profiles for hazard X?  (or: Who is most

Q: What is the strongest model in Energy?

Q: Are there country differences in overall Energy concern?

   confirmed predictor under any hazard, say:
   "The variable X was considered by the model but was not confirmed
   as a predictor of any Energy hazard in this study."

Q: How was concern measured?
A: Each hazard was scored as Likelihood (1 to 5) multiplied by Impact
   (1 to 5), giving a 1 to 25 score.  A score of 12 or above is
   classified as high concern.

Q: What is the sample size?  Who took part?
A: 407 respondents in total: 149 from Germany, 129 from Italy, 96 from
   Spain, 22 from Portugal, and 11 from Ireland.

Q: What does "confirmed predictor" mean?
A: A confirmed predictor is one that survived four statistical filters:
   (1) LASSO penalised selection, (2) likelihood-ratio testing in the
   full multivariate model, (3) Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at
   q=0.05, and (4) the events-per-predictor cap to avoid overfitting.
   Only confirmed predictors should be reported as findings.

Q: Can I say X causes Y?
A: No.  This is an observational, cross-sectional analysis.  Use
   associational language only:  "is associated with", "predicts higher
   odds of", "is linked to".

Q: What about Housing or Transport?

SECTION 10.  TECHNICAL CAVEATS
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A.  Step 5 LRT tests a predictor in the FULL multivariate context.
    Step 7 ANOVA tests it in ISOLATION.  Disagreement between them is
    expected and not a bug.

B.  Post-LASSO p-values are slightly anti-conservative.  Treat them
    as approximate indicators of association rather than exact
    Type I error rates.

C.  ONE EPP-flagged effect in Energy: solar_install_likelihood "Not
    sure" in the missing_savings model (n too small for stable
    standard error estimation).  Disclose this when reporting that
    odds ratio.

D.  Borderline evidence-strength predictors (those marked "post-LASSO
    bias") are real but should be cited with the caveat that the
    p-value is approximate.
