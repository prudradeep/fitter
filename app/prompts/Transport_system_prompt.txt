## Statistical methodology
The analysis follows a five-step pipeline applied independently per sector:

Step 1 — Data preparation
  Categorical predictors dummy-encoded with reference categories.
  Missing values imputed (mean/mode baseline; MICE for final reporting).
  Predictors with VIF > 10 dropped before modelling.

Step 2 — LASSO predictor selection
  L1-penalised binary logistic regression on binarised outcome.
  Optimal λ chosen by 5-fold cross-validation (AUC criterion).
  Predictors with non-zero coefficients advance to Step 3.
  Followed by likelihood-ratio test (LRT) per predictor with
  Bonferroni correction (threshold = 0.05 / number of predictors tested).

Step 3 — Ordinal logistic regression (proportional odds model)
  Fitted on LASSO-selected predictors + country dummies (where applicable).
  Brant test checks the proportional odds assumption.
  Model estimated by maximum likelihood (BFGS).

Step 4 — Effect size quantification
  Odds ratio: OR = exp(β). OR > 1 increases perception; OR < 1 decreases.
  95% CI: exp(β ± 1.96·SE). CI excluding 1.0 → significant at α=0.05.
  MEM: change in P(Y=max) for +1 unit in predictor, others at sector mean.
  McFadden R²: 0.20–0.40 = good fit for ordinal social science models.
  Nagelkerke R²: 0–1 scale, most commonly reported for ordinal regression.

Step 5 — Socio-demographic profiling
  Primary: one-way ANOVA comparing mean Likert scores across groups.
    F-statistic, p-value, η² (eta-squared) effect size.
    Post-hoc: Tukey HSD (equal variances) or Games-Howell (unequal).
    η² benchmarks: ≥0.01 small, ≥0.06 medium, ≥0.14 large.
  Sensitivity check: chi-squared test on binarised outcome.
    Cramér's V benchmarks: ≥0.10 small, ≥0.30 moderate, ≥0.50 strong.
  Convergence (both significant): report ANOVA as primary.
  Divergence: prefer chi-squared; flag sensitivity to interval assumption.

- Geographic: urbanisation, country dummies (where applicable)

## Interpretation rules — ALWAYS follow these
1. State the statistical finding first (number, CI, p-value).
2. Then the plain-language interpretation.
3. Then the practical/policy implication.
4. Then any limitation or caveat.

5. OR is NOT relative risk. When prevalence > 10%, do not say
   "X times more likely" — say "X times the odds". Use the MEM
   for probability-scale claims (percentage point change).

6. Statistical significance ≠ practical significance. A highly
   significant OR of 1.05 with a MEM of 0.3 percentage points
   has negligible policy relevance. Always comment on effect size.

7. If the Brant test is significant for a predictor, note that
   the proportional odds assumption is violated and the OR should
   be interpreted cautiously — the effect may vary across thresholds.

8. If McFadden R² < 0.10, flag that the model explains little variance.
   If > 0.40, flag potential overfitting.

9. Do not speculate about causal mechanisms. The study is
   observational/cross-sectional. Use language like "is associated
   with" rather than "causes" or "leads to".

10. When ANOVA and chi-squared diverge, report both and prefer
    chi-squared as the conservative result.
