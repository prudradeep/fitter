Evaluate the mitigation measure using the following criteria:

1. Hazard Fit
- The mitigation measure should reasonably help prevent, reduce, adapt to, or mitigate the selected hazard.
- It does not need to solve the hazard completely.
- If the relationship is possible but unclear, return NEEDS_CLARIFICATION.

2. Sector Fit
- The mitigation measure should primarily belong to the selected sector.
- Some overlap with other sectors is acceptable.
- Reject only if it clearly belongs to another sector.

3. Country and Region Fit
- The mitigation measure should be appropriate for the selected country and region.
- Generic European policy measures are acceptable.
- Reject only if it explicitly refers to another country, region, programme, or institution that is incompatible with the selected location.

4. European Twin Transition Policy Alignment
The mitigation measure should reasonably support one or more objectives of European Twin Transition policies.

Examples include:
- energy efficiency
- renewable energy
- building renovation
- electrification
- smart energy systems
- sustainable mobility
- public transport
- EV charging infrastructure
- climate resilience
- circular economy
- digitalisation
- AI, IoT and smart technologies
- digital public services
- consumer protection
- affordability
- Just Transition
- support for vulnerable populations

The mitigation measure does NOT need to reference a specific EU regulation or funding programme.

5. Policy Quality

The mitigation measure should describe a meaningful intervention or action.

Examples:
- Introduce grants
- Provide subsidies
- Expand public transport
- Upgrade electricity grids
- Deploy smart meters
- Improve charging infrastructure
- Fund building retrofits

Very short but meaningful mitigation measures are acceptable.

Reject only when the mitigation measure:
- is unrelated to the hazard,
- clearly belongs to another sector,
- is geographically inconsistent,
- is unrelated to European Twin Transition objectives,
- or is not a mitigation measure.
