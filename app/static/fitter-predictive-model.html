How to read a model result

Result tables, FITTER-EU D4.4

Each result links one group of people to one hazard. Seven values describe it. Here is what each one means.

A

Who is compared

Which people is the result about?
Example characteristic: behind on utility bills ReferenceNever Once Twice or more

Every answer to a question is compared with one reference answer, usually the most common one. A result for “Twice or more” always means: compared with people who answered “Never”.

Everything else the model knows (age, income, home, country) is held equal, so the difference belongs to this one characteristic.
B

Odds ratio

Which way, and how strongly?

Above 1: the group is more concerned. Below 1: less concerned. 1: no difference.

2.5 means 2.5 times the odds of being at a higher concern level. 0.5 means half the odds.

C

95% range

How sure can we be of the odds ratio?

The survey asked some people, not everyone. Asking different people would give a slightly different odds ratio. The true value is very likely (95%) inside the range.

Narrow range: the size is precise. Wide range: the size is uncertain.

D

Change in very high concern

How much more likely, in everyday numbers?
Without ita typical person 12% With itsame person, only this changed 30%

The model takes a typical respondent and predicts their chance of being in the very high concern level (the top fifth). It then switches on the characteristic, keeps everything else the same, and predicts again.

The difference is in percentage points: 12% to 30% is +18 points.

E

How much to trust it

Is the link real and repeatable?

Borderlinep up to 0.05, use with care

F

Small-group warning

Are there enough people to size the effect?
–No flag: enough people, the size can be relied on
⚠Flag: few people in the group. Trust the direction, not the exact size
Fewer than 5 events per factor
G

Effect size: does it matter in practice?

A result can be real (E) but small. B and D size one group; these two size the whole characteristic.

Share of differences explained

People differ in how concerned they are. This is the share of those differences that the characteristic explains on its own. Medium or large effects are the ones worth acting on.

η² (eta-squared)Cohen benchmarks

Gap between groups

The difference in average concern score (1 to 25) between the most and least concerned answers. A bigger gap means a more visible difference.

Putting it together: an example

Illustrative values, not a real result

Hazard: Higher electricity bills. Characteristic: behind on utility bills.

ATwice or morevs “Never”

People who fell behind twice or more, compared with otherwise similar people who never did.

B2.5odds ratio

Above 1, so they are more concerned: 2.5 times the odds of a higher concern level.

C1.6 – 3.995% range

The whole range is above 1, so they are clearly more concerned. The true odds ratio is somewhere between 1.6 and 3.9.

D+18 pts12% → 30%

For a typical person, falling behind on bills raises the chance of very high concern from 12% to 30%.

EStrongdoubly robust, both

The link is real, repeats in new samples, and holds for this hazard and the whole sector.

FNo flagsmall-group warning

A medium effect: large enough to matter in practice, not just statistically.

In plain words: people who repeatedly fall behind on their bills are clearly, reliably and noticeably more worried about higher electricity bills than people who do not.
