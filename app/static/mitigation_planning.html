What is
Mitigation Planning?

In a twin transition policy context, mitigation planning is the process of identifying, assessing and designing measures to prevent or reduce the negative impacts (hazards) that may arise when a green and/or digital transition policy is implemented.

How does mitigation planning work?

1. Identify hazards

Understand potential negative impacts of the policy.

2. Analyse risks

Assess who could be affected and how serious the impact could be.

3. Design mitigation measures

Develop solutions to prevent or reduce the negative impacts.

4. Implement and monitor

Put measures in place and monitor their effectiveness, adjusting as needed.

Key elements of mitigation planning

Targeted

Focuses on specific hazards and affected groups.

Preventive and responsive

Aims to prevent impacts where possible and reduce them where they cannot be avoided.

Inclusive

Considers the needs of vulnerable and disadvantaged groups.

Evidence-based

Uses data, stakeholder input and policy analysis to design effective measures.

What does mitigation planning achieve?

Reduced negative impacts

Minimises harm to people, the economy, the environment and services.

Fairer transition

Supports vulnerable groups and prevents new inequalities.

More successful policy outcomes

Helps achieve green and digital transition goals in a sustainable and inclusive way.

Greater resilience

Builds a more resilient society and economy that can adapt to change.
